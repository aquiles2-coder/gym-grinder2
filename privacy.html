Privacy Policy

Last updated: 14 September 2026

This page explains how Gym Grinder handles personal data. It is written for users in the European Union / EEA and the United Kingdom. It is not legal advice.

1. Who is responsible

The data controller is the operator of Gym Grinder (the person who runs this website and the linked Firebase project).

Contact for privacy requests: use the contact method published by the operator on GitHub (gym-grinder2) or the email they provide on this site. Replace this line with a real contact address before going live in Europe.

If the operator is not established in the EU and offers the service to people in the EU, an EU representative may need to be named here (GDPR Art. 27).

2. What this app is

Gym Grinder is a gamified workout tracker. You can create an account, log strength and cardio sets, gain XP and levels, track muscle progress, create shared workout “Trains”, and appear on leaderboards.

3. What data we process

Depending on how you use the app, we may process:

  • Account data: email (via Firebase Authentication), password (stored by Firebase, not in readable form on this site), nickname, approval status, session id for single-device login.
  • Workout data: exercise name, weight, reps, cardio distance, timestamps, XP, level, strength stat, muscle-progress values, weekly progress, history of recent sets.
  • Trains you create: train name, list of exercises, and that you created them (they can be used by other players).
  • Public leaderboard data: nickname, level, XP and related ranking fields visible to other logged-in players.
  • Technical data: data Firebase / Google needs to run Auth and Firestore (for example IP address and device/browser information processed by Google as part of their service).

We do not ask for medical diagnoses, heart-rate from wearables, photos of your body, payment cards, or government ID.

4. Is workout data “health data”?

Weight, training history and muscle progress can be treated as data concerning health under GDPR Article 9, because they relate to your body and physical activity. Gym Grinder is a fitness tracker, not a medical service. To stay on the safe side for users in Europe, we treat workout logs as special-category data and rely on your explicit consent for that processing, in addition to the contract basis for running your account.

5. Why we process data (purposes and legal bases)

  • Create and run your account, log sets, calculate XP/levels, show history and week progress — performance of a contract (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)).
  • Store workout / body-related logs — explicit consent (Art. 6(1)(a) and Art. 9(2)(a)). You can withdraw consent; the account may then no longer work as a tracker.
  • Admin approval of new accounts — contract and legitimate interest in keeping the community invite-only (Art. 6(1)(b) and (f)).
  • Single-device login (one active session; a new login signs the old device out) — contract and security legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(b) and (f)).
  • Public leaderboards — your choice to use a feature that publishes nickname, level and XP to other players; treat this as consent / contract for that feature. You can ask to be removed from the board by deleting your account.
  • Shared Trains — contract: trains you publish are meant to be used by other players until you delete them or your account.

6. Who receives the data

We do not sell your data.

  • Google Firebase (Authentication and Cloud Firestore) processes data as our processor to host accounts and the database.
  • Other players can see leaderboard fields and trains you marked as shared.
  • The operator can see user documents in the Firebase console in order to approve accounts and keep the app running.

7. Transfers outside the EU

Firebase is operated by Google. Data may be stored or accessed in the United States or other countries. Transfers rely on Google’s Data Processing Agreement and Standard Contractual Clauses (and any extra measures Google documents). This involves a different legal environment than the EU.

8. How long we keep data

  • Account and progress data: for as long as the account exists.
  • Set history: the app keeps a limited recent history (currently the newest 30 sets) and a running total of XP / levels.
  • After you delete your account we remove your user document, stored sets, trains you created, and your leaderboard row. Firebase Auth deletion may need a recent login.
  • Backups on Google’s side follow Firebase / Google retention, which we do not fully control.

9. Your rights

If GDPR or UK GDPR applies to you, you can ask to:

  • access your data;
  • correct inaccurate data;
  • delete your data (“right to be forgotten”) — use Delete account in the app header when the app is online, or contact the operator;
  • restrict or object to certain processing;
  • receive a copy of the data you provided (portability);
  • withdraw consent without affecting processing that already happened;
  • lodge a complaint with your national data protection authority (for example CNPD in Portugal, AEPD in Spain, CNIL in France, ICO in the UK).

10. Cookies and similar technology

The static pages do not set marketing cookies. When the app is online, Firebase Auth uses local storage / similar browser storage so you stay signed in. That storage is needed to run the service. If analytics or advertising scripts are added later, this section must be updated and non-essential cookies will need consent.

11. Children

Gym Grinder is not directed at children under 16. Do not create an account if you are under that age.

12. Security

Access is protected by Firebase Authentication. Firestore rules are meant to stop other players from changing your private document or the approval flag. No online service is perfectly secure. If we become aware of a personal-data breach, we will handle it under the GDPR 72-hour notification duty where it applies.

13. Automated decisions

XP, level and muscle scores are calculated from the sets you log. That is game logic, not a legal or similarly significant automated decision under Art. 22 GDPR.

14. Changes

If this policy changes in a material way, the date at the top will be updated. Continued use after a change means you should read the new text.
